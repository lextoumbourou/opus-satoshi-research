# What time zone were Satoshi's computers set to?

Status: working notes, 2026-09-26. Labels: **VERIFIED** (primary artefact checked by me), **REPORTED**, **INFERENCE** (with confidence).

This note is about **machine settings** (clocks, time zones), which are different from **activity patterns** (when he posted). Activity patterns have been studied to death (Stefan Thomas 2011, Lopp, Chain Bulletin 2020, Blackburn et al. 2022). Machine settings much less so.

### Same-message check: Date offset vs Message-ID time (VERIFIED)

For the same message, Thunderbird writes the Message-ID (hex UTC seconds from `PR_Now()`) and the `Date:` header (local time + OS offset) at the moment of sending. Where both survive (Malmi's or the list archive's copies, via `data/satoshi/posts.jsonl`), **local time − offset equals the Message-ID time to the second**:

| p2p-research | Fri, 13 Feb 2009 02:31:20 +0000 | `<4994DB78.5080602@gmx.com>` | 2009-02-13 02:31:20Z |

Also `Sat, 24 Oct 2009 00:55:06 +0100` (bitcoin-list, a day before the EU switch). SourceForge's arrival times lag by 16 s to 28 min, so his clock was right.

Conclusion (VERIFIED mechanism, INFERENCE on meaning): Satoshi used Thunderbird with his GMX account **from at least 11 Feb 2009** (the P2P Foundation posts) to Dec 2010. The OS time zone of that machine followed UK rules the whole time.

## Inference

INFERENCE (medium-high): from January 2009 to at least February 2011, the computers Satoshi used to build releases and send email were set to **UK time**: GMT in winter and BST in summer, switching on the EU dates, with accurate clocks. The only outliers are the two white paper PDFs (US offsets, Oct 2008 and Mar 2009). Those were made by OpenOffice on a Windows machine with MS Office fonts, possibly a different machine, VM or deliberate setting.

What the UK-time setting does and doesn't mean:
- It is **consistent with** a UK/Irish/Portuguese resident, and with the independent linguistic clues (British spellings, "bloody", the Times front-page headline in the genesis block).
- It is **not proof of residence**. A careful person could set a machine to London time as cover. But a decoy would more naturally be UTC or a random zone. Keeping a correct, DST-following Western-European zone for two years, on accurate clocks, is what a machine set to its owner's local time looks like.
- It sits awkwardly with the **activity pattern**. Satoshi was almost never active 05:00–11:00 UTC; in UK time that means sleeping roughly 05:00–13:00, a strong night owl (Chain Bulletin plotted this). In US Eastern time it's a normal midnight-to-6am sleep. So either a UK night owl, or a non-UK person whose machines were set to UK time.

## Relevance to named candidates (INFERENCE)

- **Adam Back.**
  - His Gmail reply attributions in Aug 2008 (COPA exhibit AB1, "On Wed, Aug 20, 2008 at 6:30 PM", matching the exhibit's UTC+01:00 rendering) fit UK time. VERIFIED quote; inference medium.
  - But his own list email of **24 Mar 2010** carries `Date: Wed, 24 Mar 2010 00:23:10 +0100` (metzdowd pipermail, VERIFIED). The UK was still on GMT (+0000) until 28 Mar 2010, so +0100 means **Central European time**, consistent with the NYT's statement that Back "moved to Malta in 2009". He sent it with **mutt** (attribution format in his 2010 posts), so the offset is the TZ of the machine running mutt, which could be a remote server.
  - In the same month, Satoshi's mail machine was on +0000 (e.g. 6 Mar 2010).
  - **Raw randombit pipermail archives** (Wayback copies of lists.randombit.net, `data/lists/randombit/pipermail/`) show all of Back's posts Nov 2010 – Feb 2011 at **+0100** (e.g. `Mon, 8 Nov 2010 17:41:51 +0100`, `Wed, 16 Feb 2011 04:49:38 +0100`) and June 2011 at **+0200**. That is CET/CEST, consistent with Malta. There is one exception, **23 Dec 2010 at +0000**, which looks like a Christmas trip to the UK: his machine's zone followed his physical location. VERIFIED.
  - **Same-day contrasts.** 1 Dec 2010: Satoshi → Malmi `00:58:37 +0000`, Back → list `18:53:53 +0100`. 25 Jan 2011: Satoshi → Malmi `18:34:03 +0000`, Back → list `23:59:24 +0100`. VERIFIED.
  - **Back's machine zone tracks his location over 12 years** (all his own `Date:` headers, VERIFIED): UK +0000/+0100 (2001–02, metzdowd); North American Eastern −0400/−0500 (Jul 2004 and May 2004 cypherpunks raw with `User-Agent: Mutt/1.4.1i`; 2007 metzdowd and cypherpunks, `Mutt/1.4.2.1i`), consistent with his Montreal years (NYT: moved to Montreal 1999); UK in Aug 2008 (Gmail attribution, inference); CET/CEST 2010–2013 (Malta; `Mutt/1.5.21` in 2013); UK +0000 at Christmas 2010. He consistently used mutt, whose Date header comes from the local machine clock/zone. Raw cypherpunks copies are in `data/lists/cypherpunks-raw/` (source: github.com/cryptoanarchywiki/2000-to-2016-raw-cypherpunks-archive). The list was nearly dead 2007–2012 (1 Back post), so it can't date the 2009 move to Malta.
  - So if Back were Satoshi, he ran two differently configured machines. His own followed his location (Malta, and the UK at Christmas); the Satoshi one was kept on UK time for at least 2010–11. That's possible for a careful person, but it's an extra assumption the NYT case doesn't address. It's also an odd choice of cover for a Briton in Malta, since it points at where he's from. Confidence that this is evidence against a single-machine Back: high; against Back overall: low–medium (a deliberate second machine is easy).
- **Hal Finney.** His machines were on Pacific time (tar headers show UTC−8; his Gmail displayed Pacific). The Satoshi machines were on UK time. For Finney-as-Satoshi you'd need deliberate London clocks on the Satoshi machines. Weak evidence against.
- **Len Sassaman** (Leuven, Belgium): CET/CEST, not UK time. Same caveat.
- **Nick Szabo, Wei Dai, Peter Todd** (North America): same caveat.
- Anyone living in the UK or Ireland in 2009–2011 fits without extra assumptions. That's a large population, so this is a filter, not an identification.
