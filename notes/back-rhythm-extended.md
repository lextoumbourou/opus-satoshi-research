# Adam Back's posting rhythm in four venues (1996–2021) vs Satoshi

Scripts: `scripts/fetch_back_activity.py` (fetch and cache) and `scripts/back_rhythm_extended.py` (analysis; reads the cache only). Output: `data/candidates/back-activity/rhythm-extended.txt`. Data and sources: `data/candidates/back-activity/README.md`. This extends `scripts/hour_compare.py`, which used only the 156 randombit posts.

## Method

- **Back's timestamps.** Every source gives a trustworthy UTC instant.
  - **randombit Cryptography list**, 2010–15: n=156. Date header with its offset (existing data).
  - **bitcointalk**, user adam3us (uid 101601), 2013–21: n=399 of the 404 on his profile. Source: the ninjastic.space API.
    - Four posts were checked against bitcointalk.org's guest view and match to the second.
    - The guest view is UTC: the profile page's "Local Time" matched `date -u` at fetch time. VERIFIED.
  - **bitcoin-dev**, 2013–21: n=197. Back's own Date header with its offset, from the gnusha.org public-inbox mirror.
  - **cypherpunks**, 1996–98: n=703 unique. Time taken from Back's own sendmail Message-ID, `<YYYYMMDDhhmm.QAAnnnnn@server.test.net>` or `@server.eternity.org`, from the cryptoanarchy.wiki archive.
    - That archive's Date headers are list-server times, often mislabelled, so they are not used.
    - Excluded: 30 posts whose Message-ID was generated by toad.com, and 14 early-1996 posts from Exeter hosts with a different ID format whose zone could not be checked.
- **Satoshi reference.** The `dst_likelihood.py` convention: n=902 timed items, 2008-08-20 → 2011-04-26, explicit or verified zone basis only.
  - The quietest 8-hour UTC window is **06:00–14:00, with 3.0% of items**. The earlier 2.8% figure was for the 2008–2010 subset.
  - Using the hour_compare set instead (n=919) gives 4.4% at 05:00–10:59 UTC, as before.
- **Statistics, per dataset and pooled:**
  - share of posts at 05:00–10:59 and 06:00–13:59 UTC, with Wilson 95% CIs;
  - mean log-likelihood per post of Back's UTC hour under a circular Gaussian KDE of Satoshi's hours (bandwidth 0.9 h, as in `dst_likelihood.py`), compared with Satoshi's own leave-one-out value and with a uniform distribution;
  - dLL: the same quantity minus Back's leave-one-out value under his own KDE;
  - overlap of the 24-bin hour histograms (OVL);
  - two-sample circular Watson U² with a 2,000-label permutation p-value;
  - hour histograms in UTC and in Back's local civil time (Europe/London for 1996–98, Europe/Malta for 2010+);
  - a best time-shift search, and a weekday/weekend split;
  - Back items inside Satoshi's active period, and minimum gaps to Satoshi items.

## 1. The 1996–98 Message-ID time is GMT (VERIFIED, high confidence)

Three independent checks:

**(a) Back's own Date headers.**
- The venona archive (the Algebra.COM node, via Wayback) keeps the sender's own Date header. I fetched 43 of Back's posts there: 24 from BST months and 19 from GMT months, across 1996–98.
- Every one matches the Message-ID time of the same post in the cryptoanarchy.wiki copy to within 0–1 minute, once the Date header is converted to UTC. Restricting to the 29 matches with only one candidate post gives the same result.
- If the Message-ID were local time, the summer posts would be off by 60 minutes.

Examples:

| Back's Date header | Message-ID |
|---|---|
| `Sun, 14 Jul 1996 19:28:19 +0100` | `<199607141828.TAA00441@server.test.net>` |
| `Fri, 2 Aug 1996 01:34:39 +0100` | `<199608020034.BAA02423@server.test.net>` |
| `Sun, 3 Aug 1997 02:42:41 +0100` | `<199708030142.CAA13821@server.test.net>` |
| `Fri, 10 Jul 1998 00:11:14 +0100` | `<199807092311.AAA07749@server.eternity.org>` |
| `Fri, 6 Dec 1996 10:27:02 GMT` | `<199612061027.KAA00273@server.test.net>` |

**(b) The sendmail queue-ID letter.**
- The first letter of the queue ID encodes the hour (A=00 … X=23).
- In all 450 posts from UK summer, the letter is exactly **one hour ahead** of the Message-ID hour, e.g. `1828.TAA` = 18:28 GMT but letter T = 19. In all 253 posts from UK winter, the two agree.
- So the queue letter follows UK civil time and the Message-ID time stays on GMT. This matches sendmail 8.x, which fills `$t` with gmtime() and takes the queue letter from localtime(); that part is from memory of the source and is corroborative only.

**(c) Arrival at the list.**
- Archive arrival minus Message-ID time has a median of 50–51 minutes in both seasons.
- The only large negatives are Sep–Oct 1998 posts, where the archive's "+0800" dates are about 12–13 hours too early (an archive mislabel).
- Side result, VERIFIED: **Back's 1996–98 mail machine ran on Europe/London time with UK DST.** His Date headers show `+0100` in summer and `GMT` in winter, and the queue-letter shift appears in every post. That he was physically in the UK is an INFERENCE (high confidence), consistent with his Exeter addresses and the NYT's account that he moved to Montreal in 1999.

## 3. UK-era shape in local time

Shares by local time of day (hours are local civil time):

| | 00–06 | 06–09 | 09–13 | 13–18 | 18–24 | quietest 8 h (local) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Satoshi, in London time | 25.1 | 1.8 | **0.3** | 18.8 | 54.0 | 07–15 (2.1%) |
| Back 1996–98, London time | 17.2 | 4.0 | **18.5** | 17.2 | 43.1 | 02–10 (13.2%) |
| Back 2010–21, Malta time | 17.2 | 2.3 | **20.3** | 31.8 | 28.5 | 02–10 (11.4%) |

- **UK-based Back in the 1990s was a late-evening poster but not a "never in the morning" one** (VERIFIED).
  - His busiest hours were 21:00–00:59 London (7.3–9.7% per hour).
  - He was nearly silent 04:00–07:59 (0.3–0.7% per hour).
  - He posted steadily from 09:00 through the afternoon (about 4–5% per hour).
- Satoshi, in London time, is effectively absent 07:00–14:59 (2.1% of items in those 8 hours; 0.3% at 09–13).
- INFERENCE (moderate confidence): Back's natural sleep gap sits at roughly 03–08 local. Satoshi's silent block is about 5 hours later and much more complete: Back never has an 8-hour window below 7% in any venue, while Satoshi's is 2–3%.
- **Weekends don't explain the difference.** Share of posts at 07:00–14:59 local:

  | | weekdays | weekends |
  |---|---|---|
  | Satoshi (London time) | 1.2% | 4.7% |
  | Back 1996–98 | 26.9% | 35.7% |
  | Back 2010–21 | 34.2% | 38.5% |

- **The shapes don't line up at any time shift either.**
  - The shift of Back's local hours that best fits Satoshi's London-time KDE is about +4.5 to +4.75 h, as if Back lived in US Eastern time.
  - Even at that shift the fit is only −3.34 (1996–98) and −3.18 (2010–21) per post. That is at or below uniform, against Satoshi's own −2.74.
  - The histogram-overlap optimum lands on a different shift (+1 h and +6 h respectively).
  - INFERENCE: the difference is in concentration as well as phase. Back posts across most of the clock (his quietest 8 hours still hold 7–13% of posts); Satoshi's record has a sharp 8-hour hole.

## 4. Satoshi's active period (Nov 2008 – Apr 2011)

- In these venues, Back posted only on randombit during this period: 15 posts. His bitcointalk and bitcoin-dev activity starts in 2013.
- **5 of the 15 fall inside Satoshi's 06:00–13:59 UTC quiet window**: 2010-11-19 08:18, 2010-11-20 10:03 and 10:09, 2010-11-21 08:33, and 2011-01-26 13:41 UTC. The earlier note counted 4 because it used the 05–11 window.
- Closest Back/Satoshi pair: Back 21:19 / Satoshi 22:46 UTC on 21 Nov 2010, 87 minutes apart. Next closest: 212, 266 and 324 minutes.
- **No direct conflict.** No pair is close enough to rule out one person, and the 20 Nov sequence (Satoshi 02:12 → Back 10:03 → Satoshi 17:24) fits one person who slept in between. VERIFIED data; INFERENCE on meaning.
- Before the period, Back's own emails to Satoshi (COPA exhibit AB1) were sent at 12:55:59 UTC on 21 Aug 2008 (inside the window) and 18:17:17 UTC. The second is a reply 17.5 minutes after Satoshi's 17:59:49 email, so its closeness means nothing.

## Conclusions

1. **Back's public posting rhythm differs strongly from Satoshi's in every venue and era tested**: 1996–98 UK, 2010–15 randombit, and 2013–21 bitcointalk and bitcoin-dev.
   - 28–49% of Back's posts fall in Satoshi's 06–14 UTC quiet window, against 3% of Satoshi's.
   - Satoshi's rhythm explains Back's hours worse than a flat distribution.
   - VERIFIED data; the statistical difference is high-confidence.
2. **The UK-era Back (1996–98) shows a daytime-plus-late-evening pattern, not Satoshi's night-owl, never-before-3pm pattern.** In London time, 18.5% of his posts fall at 09–13 against Satoshi's 0.3%. VERIFIED.
3. **As identity evidence, this is weak evidence against Back = Satoshi** (INFERENCE, low–moderate confidence).
   - For one person to produce both records, "Satoshi" must have been kept almost entirely to about 15:00–07:00 London (16:00–08:00 Malta), including weekends, for 2.5 years.
   - Meanwhile the same person posted under his own name in the mornings, including on 19–21 Nov 2010 while Satoshi was active.
   - That is possible for a deliberate or day-job-constrained pseudonym, so it doesn't exclude him.
   - Neither does it support him: his natural rhythm gives no reason to expect Satoshi's hours.
4. **The Message-ID method works** and exposes Back's 1990s machine zone (Europe/London with DST). VERIFIED. It can be reused for other 1990s sendmail users.

## Caveats

- **Pseudonym discipline.** A single person can deliberately confine a pseudonym to chosen hours. A rhythm mismatch is therefore weak evidence either way, and a match would also have been weak.
- **Different eras.** None of the four datasets covers 2008–09. Back's rhythm then is observed only through 2 emails (Aug 2008) and 15 randombit posts (2010–11). Habits change over 10–15 years.
- **Venue and topic mix.**
  - Forum posts, list emails and (for Satoshi) SVN commits behave differently.
  - The bitcoin-dev and bitcointalk peaks (2013, 2015) coincide with specific debates and travel.
  - Local-time histograms for 2010+ assume Malta, and Gmail offsets show travel. UTC statistics are unaffected.
- **Circularity.** The 06–14 UTC window was chosen as Satoshi's quietest, which flatters the gap. The Watson U², KDE and OVL comparisons don't depend on that choice.
- **Coverage gaps.**
  - ninjastic lacks 5 of Back's 404 bitcointalk posts.
  - 14 early-1996 Exeter-host posts were excluded.
  - The venona check covers 43 posts in 8 sampled months; the queue-letter check covers all 703.
- **Permutation p-values** are floored at 0.0005 by the 2,000 permutations.
